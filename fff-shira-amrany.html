FinTech Female Fridays: Meet Shira Amrany

Shira Amrany Data & Analytics · Indagari
Manvir Singh · Jul 10 · 4 min

Shira Amrany’s career began in development, finance, and strategy within the hospitality industry, where she was drawn to growth-stage companies and the opportunity to build analytics and processes from the ground up. During the COVID-19 pandemic, she decided to learn how to code, adding technical skills to her business background. She later joined Indagari as its fourth employee, where she now helps companies answer critical business questions using consumer transaction data.

Tell us how your career started. What drew you toward growth-stage companies?

I started in development, finance, and strategy in hospitality. What I kept gravitating toward were the growth-stage companies — the ones where the analytics function didn’t exist yet and somebody had to build it.

There’s a particular kind of satisfaction in standing up a process from nothing. You’re not inheriting a dashboard someone else designed; you’re deciding what the business should be measuring in the first place.

You learned to code during the pandemic. What prompted that decision?

I kept running into the same wall: I could describe exactly what I needed from the data, but I was dependent on someone else to get it. That dependency slows everything down.

So I decided to close the gap. Learning to code didn’t turn me into an engineer — it turned me into someone who can prototype an answer before asking anyone to build it properly.

Learning to code did not make me an engineer. It made me someone who can prototype the answer before asking anyone to build it.

Shira Amrany

Joining Indagari as employee number four

Joining a company that small means the role you’re hired for and the role you end up doing are rarely the same thing. Shira came in to work on analytics and quickly found herself shaping how the product answers questions for customers.

What does your work at Indagari actually look like day to day?

We help companies answer critical business questions using consumer transaction data. In practice that means a lot of translation — taking a vague strategic question and turning it into something the data can actually speak to.

What advice would you give women earlier in their fintech careers?

Get close to the data and get close to the customer. Those two things are hard to fake and they compound. Everything else — title, team, function — moves around a lot more than people expect.

  • Build the thing that does not exist yet
  • Close your own dependencies
  • Stay close to the customer question
